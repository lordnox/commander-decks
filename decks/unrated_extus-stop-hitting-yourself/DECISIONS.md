# Decisions

## How to use

This file is the deck's memory. The primer is how to play the deck. This file is why the list, the primer, and the assessment look the way they do.

- **Cards in** — why each unique card is here and what job a replacement must keep doing.
- **Cards out** — why a card left and what replaced it. Read this before bringing a cut back.
- **Primer** — why a line, section, or win-turn claim is written the way it is.
- **Rules** — Oracle readings and stack order that were checked.
- **Talks** — dated session notes so an argument does not live only in chat.

Every unique card needs a Cards in line. The other headings appear when there is something to record.

## Goals

- **Aikido** — Invite or force attacks, then make the attacker pay for supplying the damage.
- **Table feel** — Aim for Jank 10, Fun 10, and Mean about 5 without making those numbers hard construction rules.

## Rankings

First pass: jank 10, fun 10, mean 5, Aikido 10. The deck creates unusually visible combat bargains and many stack decisions, but its nastiest finishers are tutorless and fragile.

## Cards in

### Commander, forced combat, and attack redirection

- **Extus, Oriq Overlord // Awaken the Blood Avatar** — Only the front face is part of the plan: every instant, sorcery, or copied spell retrieves a nonlegendary creature trap. Awaken the Blood Avatar is deliberately ignored.
- **Maddening Imp** — The loudest old trap: compel an opponent's whole non-Wall board to attack or destroy whatever stays home.
- **Nettling Imp** — Points one opposing creature into combat and threatens to destroy it if its controller declines.
- **Flumph** — A flying defender and political draw engine that rewards routing small packets of damage into a creature Extus can retrieve.
- **Instigator** — Discards a recoverable creature to force an entire opposing board into combat.
- **Rage Nimbus** — A flying defender that repeatedly picks an opposing creature that must attack.
- **Goblin Spymaster** — Gives each opponent a Goblin that must attack, adding bodies and pressure without building our own army.
- **Windshaper Planetar** — Flashes in after attackers are declared, sends each attacker toward a new legal defender, and returns through Extus for another combat.
- **Life of the Party** — Gives every opponent a permanently goaded copy, then grows with our odd collection of creatures when it attacks.
- **Crown of Doom** — A transferable political hot potato that rewards the table for attacking whoever currently wears it.
- **Rite of the Raging Storm** — Gives every player a disposable 5/1 trampler that cannot attack us, ensuring combat happens elsewhere.
- **Angel's Trumpet** — Forces able creatures to attack and burns players who leave untapped creatures behind.

### Disposable defenses

- **Mine Bearer** — Sacrifices to destroy an attacking creature, then waits in the graveyard for an Extus trigger.
- **Invisible Force Field** — Grants indestructible to four permanents, then rebounds for a second cast and a second Extus trigger.
- **Spurnmage Advocate** — Returns two cards to an opponent as a political price for destroying an attacker.
- **Shieldmage Advocate** — Gives an opponent one card back to prevent all damage from the most dangerous source.
- **Frontline Strategist** — Morphs into a one-sided combat fog against non-Soldiers without advertising itself in advance.
- **Standard Bearer** — Pulls any opposing targeted spell or ability toward itself when that target remains legal.
- **Muck Drubb** — Madness and flash turn it into an ambush that steals a single-target spell aimed at another creature.
- **Kami of False Hope** — Sacrifices to prevent combat damage for a turn, then returns through magecraft.
- **Children of Korlis** — Refunds life lost during an Eye for an Eye, Volcano Hellion, or Coalhauler turn.
- **Inkshield** — Converts one lethal combat step into an evasive army for the return swing.
- **Batwing Brume** — Fogs combat while charging the attacker one life per attacking creature.
- **Kor Haven** — Uses a land slot to blank one attacker without committing another spell.

### Damage routing and reflection

- **Shaman en-Kor** — Builds one-point replacement shields that route damage into Mogg Maniac, Spitemare, Stuffy Doll, or another reflector.
- **Mogg Maniac** — Sends damage dealt to it into a player; cheap enough to recur and redeploy repeatedly.
- **Spitemare** — Reflects its damage to any target, turning a block or deliberate burn into removal or reach.
- **Boros Reckoner** — A compact reflector whose hybrid cost fits every colored source combination in the deck.
- **High Priest of Penance** — Converts any damage dealt to it into destruction of a target nonland permanent, fixing the deck's interaction blind spot on an Extus-recurable body.
- **Brash Taunter** — Indestructible reflector that can fight a large opposing creature to manufacture damage.
- **Stuffy Doll** — Names one opponent, survives most damage, and supplies its own first point for Guilty Conscience.
- **Souls of the Faultless** — A three-mana defender that gains life and drains the attacking player for every point of combat damage it receives.
- **Martyrdom** — Gives one creature zero-cost replacement shields that can absorb damage from any creature, planeswalker, or player.
- **Kor Chant** — Turns damage from one source away from our creature and into a reflector or opposing creature.
- **Reflect Damage** — Makes the source's controller take the next damage event instead of its intended recipient.
- **Mirror Strike** — Lets an unblocked creature hit its own controller rather than us.
- **Delirium** — Taps an opposing creature, makes it hit its controller, and prevents its combat damage for the turn.
- **Arcbond** — Turns one damaged creature into the source of a table-wide damage cascade; it is the dangerous instant-speed ignition the reflector package lacked.
- **Backlash** — Instant-speed version of the large-creature self-punch.
- **Deflecting Palm** — Prevents one source's damage to us and deals the prevented amount to that source's controller.
- **Wear // Tear** — Answers an artifact, an enchantment, or both when fused while still casting an instant that triggers Extus.
- **Boros Fury-Shield** — Prevents an attacking creature's damage to us and, with red mana, hits its controller for that creature's power.
- **Imp's Mischief** — Changes a single-target spell to a new target and charges life equal to that spell's mana value.
- **Blaze of Glory** — Makes one defending creature block every attacker it legally can; an indestructible reflector can absorb an entire combat and return the damage.
- **Pain for All** — Immediately makes one creature punch another target, then turns every later damage event on that creature into damage to each opponent.
- **Guilty Conscience** — Turns Stuffy Doll into a one-opponent mandatory damage chain and makes every other reflector dangerous.
- **Repercussion** — Makes creature damage rebound onto each creature's controller, turning Crypt Rats or Spontaneous Combustion into a finisher.

### Sacrifice, recursion, and copying

- **Body Snatcher** — Discards a creature on entry, then can reanimate another creature when sacrificed while Extus returns the Snatcher itself.
- **Dualcaster Mage** — Copies an instant or sorcery at flash speed; that copy triggers Extus a second time.
- **Worthy Cause** — Sacrifices a creature for life as an additional cost, letting Extus target that same creature before the spell resolves; buyback repeats the trick.
- **Burnt Offering** — Converts a creature into black and red mana while Extus immediately retrieves the sacrifice.
- **Ritual of the Machine** — The Homelands prize: sacrifice one of our recyclable creatures to permanently steal a nonartifact, nonblack creature.
- **Skulltap** — Sacrifices a creature as an additional cost, retrieves it through Extus, then draws two cards.
- **Deadly Dispute** — Sacrifices a creature or artifact, retrieves a creature through Extus, draws two, and leaves a Treasure to help recast it.
- **Corrupted Conviction** — A second two-mana sacrifice-and-draw spell that loads Extus before its magecraft trigger chooses a target.
- **Spontaneous Combustion** — Sacrifices a creature, retrieves it through Extus, and deals 3 to every creature as a Repercussion or reflector setup.
- **Fork** — Copies any instant or sorcery and causes an additional Extus trigger without being a tutor.
- **Thrill of Possibility** — Discards a creature as an additional cost, returns it with the resulting magecraft trigger, then draws two.
- **High Market** — Sacrifices a threatened creature so a later instant or sorcery can return it through Extus.

### Creatures that cash in the machinery

- **Volcano Hellion** — Deals an arbitrarily chosen amount to itself and another creature; aim the second half at a reflector while staying above the self-damage.
- **Crypt Rats** — Scales a black-mana burst across every creature and player, which reflectors and Repercussion convert into lethal arithmetic.
- **Big Game Hunter** — Madness pairs with the discard costs and destroys a large nonblack creature on entry.
- **Bone Shredder** — Removes a nonartifact, nonblack creature and naturally dies to echo so Extus can retrieve it.
- **Humble Defector** — Political draw that can be sacrificed after activation, retrieved by Extus, and kept rather than donated.
- **Duelist's Heritage** — Gives any attacking creature double strike, encouraging attacks between opponents or turning a permitted attacker lethal.

- **Tainted Peak** — Converts a black source into efficient black-red fixing.
- **Temple of Silence** — White-black fixing whose scry helps a tutorless deck.
- **Temple of Malice** — Black-red fixing whose scry helps a tutorless deck.
- **Temple of Triumph** — Red-white fixing whose scry helps a tutorless deck.

## Cards out

- **Norritt** — Flumph took the slot because Norritt was a four-mana duplicate provocateur whose blue-creature untap mode had no targets; Flumph adds repeatable political draw.
- **Vengeful Ancestor** — Windshaper Planetar replaces proactive goad with a recurable creature that can redirect an attack already aimed at us.
- **Resistance Fighter** — Invisible Force Field protects four permanents instead of blanking one creature and rebounds for a second magecraft trigger.
- **Truefire Captain** — High Priest of Penance keeps the damaged-creature role at half the mana value and answers any nonland permanent instead of adding a seventh reflector.
- **Coalhauler Swine** — Souls of the Faultless is three mana cheaper, survives ordinary combat, and punishes only the attacking player rather than burning the whole table and us.
- **Zealous Inquisitor** — Deadly Dispute replaces an inefficient `{1}{W}`-per-point router with the card flow and redeployment mana the Extus engine needed.
- **Traitor's Roar** — Arcbond is cheaper, instant-speed, triggers Extus without conspire setup, and turns the existing reflector board into a genuine finish.
- **Eye for an Eye** — Wear // Tear gives up the unsafe symmetrical burn for necessary artifact and enchantment interaction.
- **General's Regalia** — Blaze of Glory costs one mana rather than six before its first effect and turns an indestructible reflector into a blocker for an entire attack.
- **Powerstone Minefield** — Pain for All preserves manufactured reflection without killing the deck's own one-toughness blockers whenever they enter combat.
- **Soul Link** — Corrupted Conviction becomes the third deliberate sacrifice-and-draw spell; Soul Link lost its spectacular target when Coalhauler Swine left.

## Rules

- **Additional costs and Extus.** A creature discarded or sacrificed to cast Worthy Cause, Burnt Offering, Skulltap, Deadly Dispute, Corrupted Conviction, Spontaneous Combustion, or Thrill of Possibility is in the graveyard before the spell becomes cast and magecraft triggers. That creature can be the trigger's target.
- **Copies count.** Fork and the copy made by Dualcaster Mage each trigger Extus because magecraft explicitly sees casts and copies.
- **Shaman en-Kor routing.** Each `{0}` activation creates a replacement shield for the next 1 damage to Shaman. Its `{1}{W}` ability can first route damage from another creature into Shaman; the prepared one-point shields can then route that damage onward into a reflector.
- **Volcano Hellion.** Its chosen damage cannot be prevented. Choose less damage than the current life total; Children of Korlis acts only after the damage event and cannot save a player from lethal damage.
- **Guilty Conscience and Stuffy Doll.** Once Stuffy Doll is dealt damage, its mandatory trigger damages the chosen player. Guilty Conscience then damages Stuffy Doll for the same amount, repeating until that player leaves the game or the chain is otherwise interrupted.
- **Guilty Conscience and Brash Taunter.** Brash Taunter also forms the mandatory damage chain after it is first dealt damage; unlike Stuffy Doll, it needs another source to start the loop.
- **Arcbond.** The chosen creature deals damage to every other creature and every player whenever it is dealt damage that turn. Damage prevented by another effect was not dealt and does not trigger Arcbond. The controller of the Arcbonded creature also takes the spray.
- **Pain for All.** Its trigger damages each opponent but not its controller or other creatures, making it safer than Arcbond. It only triggers when damage is actually dealt to the enchanted creature.
- **Blaze of Glory.** The targeted defending creature blocks every attacker it legally can, but restrictions and evasion still apply. It does not itself grant indestructible or change combat-damage assignment.
- **Invisible Force Field.** Rebound casts the spell from exile at the next upkeep, so Extus triggers again even if there are no useful indestructible targets.
- **Madness and Extus.** A Big Game Hunter or Muck Drubb cast for madness moves from discard to exile, not the graveyard, so that card cannot be the magecraft target from the same discard.
- **Awaken the Blood Avatar.** It is legal to cast from the command zone, but this deck intentionally treats the back face as blank.

## Talks

### 2026-08-31 — Brew lock

The user delegated the exact finishers and asked for the crazier option, creature-heavy play rather than another draw-go Lady Evangela deck, no tutors, and goals of Jank 10, Fun 10, and Mean around 5. The resulting plan is visible creature-go aikido: force combat, bargain with disposable defenders, route damage through creatures, and let tutorless two-card contraptions end games. The title is *Stop Hitting Yourself*.

### 2026-09-01 — Inspiration audit

The user supplied another Extus aikido list and asked this deck to learn from it without treating that list as categorically better. Eleven swaps remove redundant reflectors, self-hostile damage, expensive routing, and dead secondary modes. The incoming package adds three sacrifice-draw spells or creatures, broad permanent interaction, whole-combat redirection, rebound protection, and two real damage engines. Sunforger, Land Tax, fetchlands, Blasphemous Act, Star of Extinction, and unsupported tribal reflectors remain out: they would improve consistency or raw lethality by replacing the tutorless creature-go identity rather than strengthening it.
