# Decisions

## How to use

This file is the deck's memory. The primer is how to play the deck. This file is why the list, the primer, and the assessment look the way they do.

- **Cards in** — why each unique card is here and what job a replacement must keep doing.
- **Cards out** — why a card or package stayed outside the 99.
- **Primer** — why a line or section is written the way it is.
- **Rules** — Oracle readings and stack order that were checked.
- **Talks** — dated session notes so an argument does not live only in chat.

## Cards out

- **Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Lightning Greaves, and Counterspell** — Generic Commander defaults do not express exile theft; the list uses slower cards with fixing, evasion, or exile text attached.
- **Displace, Essence Flux, Ghostly Flicker, Planar Incision, and Teferi's Time Twist** — Repeating Umbris's until-land trigger is too small a reward to make blink a main package.
- **Drown in Dreams, Extract from Darkness, Curse of Unbinding, and Zellix, Sanity Flayer** — Dedicated mill builds a graveyard resource that this version subsequently exiles and distracts from theft.
- **Dream Devourer** — Foretell exiles cards owned by this deck, which never increases Umbris's power.
- **Public Enemy, Ghoulish Impetus, and Renegade Silent** — Goad adds a separate politics plan without advancing the exile bank.
- **Phyrexian Metamorph and Mirrorhall Mimic** — A normal copy of Umbris retains legendary and cannot coexist with the commander.
- **Parallel Thoughts** — Cut for Canoptek Scarab Swarm. It was a five-mana escape hatch from Shared Fate's replacement effect, but the pile is shuffled, it affects no board, and it exiles this deck's own cards, so it was the only exile effect that grew Umbris by nothing. Moonlight Bargain remains the clean way to put selected cards directly into hand.
- **Elspeth's Nightmare** — Cut for Agent of Erebos. Chapter I only answers power 2 or less and the graveyard exile arrives three turns later; the Agent exiles a whole yard immediately and repeats off constellation.
- **Muse Vessel** — Cut for Abyssal Harvester. Four mana to deploy plus three per activation took one card per turn, which was the slowest theft rate in the list.
- **Abhorrent Oculus** — Requires exiling six cards from this deck's graveyard, which it has no self-mill to supply; it is an Eye rather than a Nightmare or Horror, and exiling its own graveyard adds no Umbris power.
- **All Hallow's Eve** — Symmetrical mass reanimation telegraphed two upkeeps ahead, in a deck that empties graveyards and keeps its own nearly empty. It belongs in a self-mill reanimator variant.
- **Agatha's Soul Cauldron** — Efficient and repeatable, but it is a recognizable modern constructed staple, exiles only one card per turn, and its real payoff needs +1/+1 counters that this list never produces. House rules prefer an on-theme card over a staple without a specific argument.
- **Scrib Nibblers** — Cut for Uchuulon. Exiling one random library card per tap was slow; Uchuulon consumes a known creature card, triggers Umbris on entry, and compounds into additional bodies.
- **Jester's Scepter** — Cut for Strategic Betrayal. Five random cards rarely matched a spell in Commander, while Betrayal provides immediate removal plus a whole graveyard deposit.
- **Ornate Kanzashi** — Cut for Nautiloid Ship. Five mana per temporary top-card look was the weakest remaining theft engine; the Ship deposits a complete graveyard immediately and steals a creature later.
- **Petals of Insight** — Cut for Espers to Magicite. Its chosen mode draws three cards, so Shared Fate replaces all three draws rather than putting this deck's cards into hand; five mana for selection was not competitive with an instant-speed three-graveyard deposit.
- **Darksteel Ingot** — Cut for Coalition Relic. Indestructible was less valuable than storing a second mana for Umbris or a six-mana engine.
- **Spectral Searchlight** — Cut for Pentad Prism. Political fixing did not compensate for entering the curve at three; the Prism enables the deck's strongest turn-three commander line.
- **Vessel of Endless Rest** — Cut for Star Compass. The single card moved to the bottom is no longer needed after increasing whole-graveyard exile, while two-mana sustained ramp improves the commander's normal timing.
- **Eater of the Dead** — Its zero-mana ability only works while it is tapped and untaps it, so it eats one creature after attacking unless another card repeatedly taps it. The deck has no such tap outlet; it is not a free whole-graveyard engine by itself.
- **Stone of Erech** — Efficient, but its replacement effect prevents opposing creatures from reaching the graveyard, shutting off Abyssal Harvester, Uchuulon, and Nautiloid Ship before they can extract value.

## Primer

- **2026-08-30 — Identity.** The primer treats Shared Fate and Knowledge Pool as finite resource exchanges, not a hard lock, and treats Umbris combat as the dependable win rather than hoping opposing decks supply a finisher.

## Rules

- **Opponent-owned exile.** Umbris counts cards opponents own regardless of which player exiled them or whether they are face down. It does not count cards this deck owns, wherever they were exiled from.
- **Spending the bank.** Casting a stolen card moves it from exile to the stack immediately, reducing each Umbris's power by one. A card moved from exile to a battlefield or graveyard also stops counting.
- **Shared Fate.** Each affected player chooses which opponent's library supplies a replacement draw. Cards remain in exile if Shared Fate leaves, but its permission to look at and play them ends.
- **Abyssal Harvester.** Its target must be a creature card put into a graveyard this turn. The token copy is a Nightmare, so it triggers every Umbris on entry, and each later activation exiles the previous Nightmare token. The exiled original stays in exile and keeps counting for Umbris.
- **Agent of Erebos.** Constellation triggers on its own entry as well as every later enchantment, including the enchantment creature Ancient Cellarspawn and the Aura Kaya's Ghostform.
- **Canoptek Scarab Swarm.** Feeder Mandibles counts only artifact and land cards for tokens, but the whole graveyard is exiled and every card in it counts toward Umbris.
- **Nautiloid Ship.** Its combat trigger can return only a creature card exiled by the Ship itself. Moving that card onto the battlefield reduces Umbris by one, while every other card stays banked.
- **Uchuulon.** Every existing Uchuulon triggers separately at the end step. Each trigger needs a different creature card still available in an opponent's graveyard, then creates another Horror copy that triggers every Umbris as it enters.
- **Espers to Magicite.** The copied token becomes only an artifact, not an artifact creature. It retains copiable rules text but cannot attack or block.
- **Strategic Betrayal.** The targeted opponent chooses which creature they exile. Both that permanent and every opponent-owned card in their graveyard immediately count for Umbris.
- **Pentad Prism.** Dimir mana gives it two charge counters through sunburst. Those counters may both be removed in one turn, allowing three lands plus the Prism to cast Umbris on turn three.
- **Knowledge Pool.** Its trigger applies only to spells cast from hand. Cards cast from Shared Fate's exile do not trigger it.
- **Nonlegendary copies.** Spark Double, Irenicus's Vile Duplication, Auton Soldier, and Nanogene Conversion explicitly remove legendary. Ordinary copy effects do not.
- **Auton Soldier.** When it copies Umbris, its nonlegendary, artifact, and myriad modifications are copiable. Its myriad tokens enter as nonlegendary Umbris copies and trigger each Umbris, but none deals commander damage.
- **Nanogene Conversion.** Becoming a copy does not cause a creature to enter the battlefield, so it creates attackers but no Umbris entry triggers.
- **No loop.** The deck contains no deterministic infinite loop; every exile, theft, copy, and direct-damage line consumes cards, mana, combat steps, or permanents.

## Talks

### 2026-08-31 — Upper-edge Bracket 2 tuning

The target “2++” is represented as Bracket 2+ because the repository supports only minus, plain, and plus positions. Seven low-output slots became four compact exile payoffs and three faster mana pieces. Uchuulon replaced Scrib Nibblers; Strategic Betrayal replaced Jester's Scepter; Nautiloid Ship replaced Ornate Kanzashi; Espers to Magicite replaced Petals of Insight. Coalition Relic, Pentad Prism, and Star Compass replaced Darksteel Ingot, Spectral Searchlight, and Vessel of Endless Rest.

The correction to Petals of Insight matters: its selected mode says “draw three cards,” so Shared Fate replaces those draws. It never bypassed Shared Fate by putting cards directly into hand. Moonlight Bargain remains the clean in-deck exception.

The strengthened list remains Bracket 2+ rather than moving to Bracket 3. Its normal plan is still a telegraphed five-mana commander followed by combat, with no Game Changers, unrestricted tutor for its own library, free interaction, deterministic combo, or repeatable early win. Better two-mana ramp and whole-graveyard deposits move the representative goldfish from roughly turns eight–ten to around turns eight–nine. The exceptional line is now three lands plus Pentad Prism, Umbris, and Tasha's Hideous Laughter: Prism on turn two, Umbris on turn three, Tasha before combat on turn four. That presents lethal only if Tasha's variable exile count reaches 20 opposing cards after Umbris's own trigger, so it is possible and unlikely, not the expected pace.

### 2026-08-31 — Graveyard-exile swaps

Six candidates were checked against live Oracle text. Three came in: Canoptek Scarab Swarm, Agent of Erebos, and Abyssal Harvester, all of which convert opposing graveyards — a resource nobody at the table defends — into permanent Umbris power. Two were rejected as belonging to a different deck (Abhorrent Oculus and All Hallow's Eve both want this deck to fill its own graveyard, which it does not do). Agatha's Soul Cauldron was rejected on the house staple rule rather than on power. The searched space was `o:exile o:grave id:bu f:c o:target`, which returns 370 cards, most of them self-graveyard renew and escape costs that do nothing for Umbris.

Graveyard exile does not shrink when spent, unlike library and hand theft, so these three are pure power rather than a bank that gets consumed. The trade is a lower theft rate: the list dropped from five repeatable theft engines to four.

### 2026-08-30 — Assessment: Bracket 2+

Checked against the live Wizards Commander page (Brackets still in beta; 0 Game Changers in Brackets 1–2, up to 3 in Bracket 3) plus the October 21, 2025 barometers (Core: at least eight turns; Upgraded: at least six) and the February 9, 2026 Game Changers additions. A live Scryfall `is:gamechanger` query (53 cards, including Farewell and Biorhythm) matches nothing in the 100. No two-card infinite, extra turns, or mass land denial. Oracle checks: Tasha's Hideous Laughter is `{1}{U}{U}` and exiles until each opponent has total mana value 20 or greater, which is a large Umbris deposit rather than a mill-out; Knowledge Pool imprints three cards per player and only exchanges spells cast from hand; Shared Fate replacements do not trigger the Pool; Spark Double / Irenicus's Vile Duplication / Auton Soldier / Nanogene Conversion are the only copies that drop legendary. The repeatable plan is incremental exile into a telegraphed commander-damage or Trident/Harvest shot, which is Core, but Tasha plus the commander's always-available body is a compact one-spell inflation that can present lethal on turn five or six, which sits at the top of the bracket. Not Bracket 3: no Game Changers, no zero- or one-mana positive rocks, one opposing-library tutor, and the non-Tasha goldfish still needs several exile engines before a win. Not Bracket 2 middle: the Tasha line is a real, keepable high roll rather than a four-card pile.

### 2026-08-30 — Rebuild lock

The commander and title are **Umbris — Mental Breakdown**. The plan is exile-theft control: Shared Fate is allowed to redefine drawing, Knowledge Pool is allowed to exchange hand-cast spells, and Tasha's Hideous Laughter is allowed despite appearing in another deck. Existing decks do not reserve mechanics or cards. The house rules remain zero current Game Changers and a preference for strange, purposeful cards over Commander staples.
